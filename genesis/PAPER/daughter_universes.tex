\begin{abstract}
We study a single, consistent higher-dimensional theory in which ordinary matter can produce a spacetime region that is causally
disconnected from its parent: $D=4+n$ dimensional Einstein gravity with large extra dimensions (fundamental scale $M_D$) supplemented by
the infinite tower of quasi-topological curvature corrections of Bueno, Cano and Hennigar, which removes the black-hole singularity.
(i)~The collapse of a dust ball is glued exactly to the regular static exterior; its maximal extension is curvature-bounded (with a
mass-independent bound $K\,l^4\simeq 188$ in $D=10$), regular at both horizons and geodesically complete, and contains a white-hole region
and a new asymptotically flat universe outside $J^-(\mathcal I^+)$ of the parent, together with a finite closed daughter (the bounced
interior, whose volume grows by up to $10^{115}$ for a stellar collapse).
(ii)~Every closed universe in the theory bounces at $a\geq l$ for any perfect fluid; matter interiors accelerate only for
$N\simeq\frac{D-3}{D-1}\ln(a_{\max}/l)$ e-folds, so lasting inflation would need a vacuum as dense as the hole.
(iii)~An energy-conserving hydrodynamics code built on the generalised Misner--Sharp equations reproduces the exact bounce to $10^{-5}$ and
shows that, with pressure, inhomogeneity and shocks, a horizon forms and the matter bounces in an untrapped core with bounded curvature; in
realistic collapse the bounced core meets the envelope at the inner horizon, and in some runs enters the white-hole region through a throat.
(iv)~With real parton distributions, a $100$\,TeV collider produces such holes iff the horizon threshold lies between $\sim3.5$--$7$\,TeV
(the LHC bound, depending on the trapped fraction) and $\sim50$\,TeV; they form a trapped surface in $10^{-28}$\,s, evaporate in $10^{-26}$\,s
toward a zero-temperature remnant, and the trapped region never closes. A double-null evolution with a scalar field shows, however, that
the inner (Cauchy) horizon becomes a curvature singularity under perturbation while $\psi$ stays bounded, so the continuation of the
bounced interior beyond it is open. A bouncing interior with a scalar field reaches lasting inflation through cosmological hysteresis.
(v)~In a full simulation (Pythia, an FCC-hh detector, pile-up of $10^3$, validated on ATLAS data to $10\%$) the events are a jet
recoiling against an invisible remnant, separable from GR black holes of the same threshold with $\sim13$ selected events at $S/B=1$; the
model survives all current data (a full reinterpretation of the ATLAS monojet bins), and a profile-likelihood analysis discovers it up to
$M_{\rm ext}\simeq27$\,TeV at $85$\,TeV ($33$\,TeV at $100$\,TeV).
All key formulas and numbers are independently re-derived (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Whether a laboratory process could create a new, causally disconnected spacetime region is a question about two things at once: the
behaviour of gravity at high curvature, and the energy at which gravity becomes strong. In general relativity (GR) the first is blocked by
singularity theorems (an inflating region in an asymptotically flat spacetime must have a singularity in its past \cite{FG87}) and the second
by the Planck scale. We work in a framework that removes both obstacles within one action, and ask, in turn: does a daughter spacetime
exist globally; does it survive realistic collapse; can a collider make it; what could be measured; does it survive current data; and
how would an experiment find it.

\section{Model U}
\subsection{Action and spherical sector}
The theory is $D$-dimensional gravity with an infinite tower of quasi-topological densities \cite{BCH24}. Its spherically symmetric sector
has second-order equations, a Birkhoff theorem and Israel-type junction conditions \cite{BCHM25a,BCHM25b}, and is controlled by one
function $h$ through the generalised Misner--Sharp relation
\begin{equation}
h(\psi)=\frac{\mu}{r^{D-1}},\qquad \psi=\frac{1-(\nabla r)^2}{r^2},\qquad \mu=\frac{16\pi G_D M}{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}} .
\end{equation}
We use the Hayward tower $h=\psi/(1-\alpha\psi)$ and the Dymnikova-type tower $h=-\ln(1-\alpha\psi)/\alpha$; both bound
$\psi<1/\alpha\equiv1/l^2$. For closed FRW regions $\psi=H^2+1/a^2$ and the generalised Friedmann equation reads
$h(H^2+1/a^2)=16\pi G_D\rho/((D-1)(D-2))$ \cite{BCHMV25}.

\subsection{Regular black holes}
The static solution $f=1-r^2\psi$ has a de Sitter core $f\simeq1-r^2/l^2$ and two horizons above the extremal mass; for the Hayward tower
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm ext}=l^{D-3}\Big(\tfrac{D-1}{D-3}\Big)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}\tfrac{D-3}{2},\qquad
T=\frac{(D-3)r_+^2-(D-1)l^2}{4\pi r_+^3},
\end{equation}
so evaporation ends at a zero-temperature remnant.

\subsection{Large extra dimensions}
With $G_D=1/M_D^{n+2}$ (Dimopoulos--Landsberg convention \cite{DL01}) and $l=c_l/M_D$, the extra dimensions have radius
$R=(M_P^2/M_D^{n+2})^{1/n}/2\pi$, $10^3$--$10^6$ times larger than $1/M_D$: collider holes are $D$-dimensional and asymptotically flat. The
horizon threshold is $M_{\rm ext}=\kappa_n c_l^{\,n+1}M_D$ with $\kappa_n=32$--$56$ (Hayward) or $13$--$17$ (Dymnikova) for $n=4$--$7$.
Experiments quote the fundamental scale in the PDG convention \cite{GRW99}, $G_D=(2\pi)^n/(8\pi M_D^{n+2})$; the two are related by
$M_D^{\rm DL}=[8\pi/(2\pi)^n]^{1/(n+2)}M_D^{\rm PDG}=0.89$--$0.38\,M_D^{\rm PDG}$ for $n=2$--$6$. All $M_D$ in this paper are
Dimopoulos--Landsberg.

\section{The global daughter spacetime}
\begin{proposition}[Exact gluing]
A closed dust ball of mass parameter $C$ and boundary angle $\chi_b$ glued to the static exterior with $\mu=C\sin^{D-1}\chi_b$ satisfies
$f(R)+\dot R^2=\cos^2\chi_b$: its surface is a radial geodesic of the exterior with energy $E=\cos\chi_b$.
\end{proposition}
The maximal extension of the exterior is tiled by patches I (parent) $\to$ II (black hole) $\to$ III (de Sitter core) $\to$ IV (white hole)
$\to$ I$'$ (new universe), matched through the finite null coordinate (Fig.~\ref{fig:global}).
\begin{proposition}[Global properties]
(a) $\max K\,l^4$ is independent of the mass over $\mu=10^{-3}$--$10^8$: $29$--$34$ ($D=4$), $45$--$48$ ($D=5$), $188$ (Hayward, $D=10$),
$919$ (Dymnikova, $D=10$); the closed interior peaks at the de Sitter value $2D(D-1)/l^4$. (b) At both horizons the Kruskal metric
function has equal finite non-zero limits from both sides. (c) Radial and non-radial, timelike and null geodesics cross every region in
finite affine parameter and never terminate. (d) For $\mu>\mu_{\rm ext}$ the surface reaches I$'$ in finite proper time ($2.5$--$35\,l$);
for $\mu<\mu_{\rm ext}$ it bounces back into the parent.
\end{proposition}
The region IV\,$\cup$\,I$'$ lies outside $J^-(\mathcal I^+)$ of the parent, has its own null infinity and ADM mass $\mu$; the bounced closed
interior is a finite daughter whose volume grows by $(a_{\max}/l)^{D-1}$: $1.3\times10^5$ ($C=10^4$, $D=10$) to $8\times10^{114}$ for a
four-dimensional stellar collapse.

\section{Interiors}
\begin{proposition}[Universal bounce]
In a closed FRW region with any perfect fluid, $\dot a^2=a^2\psi-1<0$ for $a<l$, so every closed universe bounces at $a_b\geq l$.
\end{proposition}
For dust the turning points coincide with the static horizon radii; the interior accelerates for $N=\ln(a_{\rm infl}/a_b)$ e-folds with
$a_{\rm infl}^{D-1}=2\alpha C/(D-3)$ (Hayward), i.e.\ $N\to\frac{D-3}{D-1}\ln\frac{a_{\max}}{l}+\frac{1}{D-1}\ln\frac{2}{D-3}$ ($29.6$ for a stellar
hole), then decelerates and recollapses. With a vacuum term $\Lambda$ it inflates for ever iff $\Lambda>\Lambda_c(C)$,
$\Lambda_c=[2(\frac{D-3}{D-1})^{(D-1)/2}/((D-3)C)]^{2/(D-3)}$ in the low-curvature limit.
\begin{lemma}[Untrapped bounce]
At a bounce of a radial timelike worldline, $(\nabla R)^2=(n\!\cdot\!\nabla R)^2\geq0$; a bounce inside a black hole requires an untrapped
core bounded by an inner apparent horizon. (Consequently a homogeneous Einstein--Cartan ball, whose Misner--Sharp mass drops by $99\%$ at the
bounce, cannot sit in a vacuum exterior.)
\end{lemma}

\section{Beyond idealised collapse}
In comoving coordinates with $U=D_\tau R$ and $\Gamma=R'e^{-\lambda}$, the Lovelock structure of the spherical sector gives
$\Gamma^2-U^2=1-R^2\psi(\mu/R^{D-1})$, $\mu'=(D-1)\rho R^{D-2}R'$, $D_\tau\mu=-(D-1)pR^{D-2}U$, $D_\tau\Gamma=-U\Gamma p'/((\rho+p)R')$ and
\begin{equation}
D_\tau U=-\frac{\Gamma^2p'}{(\rho+p)R'}+R\psi-\frac{D-1}{2}R\psi'(y)(y+p),\qquad y=\mu/R^{D-1},
\end{equation}
which reduces to GR and to the generalised Friedmann equation. An energy-conserving Lagrangian scheme with non-homologous artificial
viscosity reproduces the exact bounce to $10^{-5}$. Inhomogeneous dust shells bounce at $R_b=\sqrt{k\alpha}$ (within $2\%$), inner shells
first. With pressure, inhomogeneity and shocks (Fig.~\ref{fig:hydro}) every run forms a horizon and bounces in an untrapped core with
$\psi\le0.994/\alpha$ and exact energy conservation. The bounced core meets the infalling envelope at the inner horizon; in some runs its
outer shells become anti-trapped next to trapped envelope shells with $\Gamma\to0$ between them --- a throat joining the parent's black-hole
region to the daughter's white-hole region. The comoving slicing cannot cross that neck; a characteristic code is needed beyond it.
Null thin shells do not exist in these theories \cite{DKS26}, so thin-shell mass-inflation arguments do not apply; regular cores of
Hayward type can be stabilised by phase-space attractors \cite{BKS21,BPS25}.

\section{Collider formation, evaporation and the daughter}
Cross-sections use NNPDF2.3 LO \cite{NNPDF23} (momentum sum $0.998$), $\hat\sigma=F_n\pi R_S^2$ with the Yoshino--Nambu factors \cite{YN03},
and the head-on trapped mass $y_n\sqrt{\hat s}$ ($y_0=1/\sqrt2$). Holes above $M_{\rm ext}$ are produced at $100$\,TeV with $30$\,ab$^{-1}$
(at least 10 events) iff $M_{\rm ext}\lesssim46$--$51$\,TeV, and are compatible with the LHC iff $M_{\rm ext}\gtrsim3.5$--$7$\,TeV
(Sec.~\ref{sec:data}): a window in $c_l$ of width $\sim0.15$--$0.25$. In the evaporating model-U Vaidya geometry $f(v,r)=1-r^2\psi(\mu(v)/r^{D-1})$ with brane Stefan--Boltzmann
emission \cite{EHM00}, $\mu-\mu_{\rm ext}\propto v^{-1}$ and $r_+-r_-\propto v^{-1/2}$: the trapped region never closes, and the bounced partons
reach the boundary of the parent's chart at finite affine parameter ($\simeq2\,l$). Formation takes $\sim10^{-28}$\,s and evaporation
$10^{-26}$--$10^{-25}$\,s. An event generator built on this physics (Fig.~\ref{fig:collider}) shows that near threshold the holes are nearly
extremal and emit almost nothing (no Hawking quantum in $76$--$85\%$ of events), the remnant carrying $\simeq95\%$ of the mass.

\section{Distinguishable predictions}\label{sec:pred}
We pass generator events through a lab frame (hole rapidity from the parton momentum fractions, leading-log initial-state radiation
$P(p_T>p)=1-\exp[-A\ln^2(\sqrt{\hat s}/p)]$, $A\simeq0.043$) and a generic detector ($|\eta|<4$, $p_T>50$\,GeV; neutrinos and a neutral remnant
invisible). At $100$\,TeV ($n=6$, $M_D=8$\,TeV, $c_l=0.6$, $M_{\rm ext}=11.5$\,TeV) model-U events have on average one visible object;
$56$--$63\%$ are a single initial-state jet balanced by the invisible remnant and $20$--$22\%$ have no visible object, with
$\langle{\rm MET}/S_T\rangle=0.94$--$0.97$. A GR hole of the same threshold and cross-section gives $2.8$ objects, median $S_T=12$\,TeV and
${\rm MET}/S_T=0.18$. The expected log-likelihood ratio of the binned (multiplicity, ${\rm MET}/S_T$) distributions separates model U from GR at
$5\sigma$ with $\sim3$ events, and the Hayward from the Dymnikova tower with $\sim660$. The four distinguishing predictions are the onset at
$M_{\rm ext}=\kappa_nc_l^{\,n+1}M_D$ rather than a few $M_D$; the invisible-remnant monojet topology; a temperature that rises from zero at
$M_{\rm ext}$; and heavy, slow ($\langle\beta\rangle\simeq0.39$) remnants (Fig.~\ref{fig:pred}). Kaluza--Klein graviton emission and species
democracy are shared by all interior classes and test only low-scale gravity and the black-hole nature of the events.

\section{Confrontation with data}\label{sec:data}
\emph{Convention.} Earlier versions of this analysis compared $M_D^{\rm DL}$ directly with experimental $M_D^{\rm PDG}$; converted, the CMS
monojet bounds $M_D^{\rm PDG}>9.9$, $7.5$, $6.3$, $5.7$, $5.3$\,TeV ($n=2$--$6$) \cite{CMS18} become $M_D^{\rm DL}>8.8$, $4.7$, $3.2$, $2.4$,
$2.0$\,TeV. All benchmarks used here are allowed.
\emph{LHC.} Near-threshold model-U events populate the monojet region. A full likelihood over the $13$ exclusive MET bins of the ATLAS
$139$\,fb$^{-1}$ search \cite{ATLAS21}, with our signal passed through the simulation of Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}, excludes $M_{\rm ext}<6.6$--$7.2$\,TeV
with full trapping and $<3.5$--$4.2$\,TeV with the head-on trapped fraction ($n=4$--$6$, $M_D^{\rm DL}=2$--$4$\,TeV); an earlier single-region
estimate with an assumed $50\%$ efficiency overstated this. Multi-object black-hole searches (high $S_T$, $\geq2$--$5$ objects) accept
$\lesssim0.4\%$ of model-U events; their limits do not apply.
\emph{Neutron stars.} A remnant captured in a neutron star accretes $\sim10^{13}$--$10^{14}$\,GeV/s and re-radiates it from
$(2$--$40)\times10^{-9}$ above extremality at $T\simeq0.16$\,GeV: there is no growth, consistent with the survival of neutron stars
(cf.~\cite{GM08}). \emph{Future machines.} With at least 10 holes (head-on trapping), the reach in $M_{\rm ext}$ is $6.3$\,TeV at the
HL-LHC, $12.7$\,TeV at $27$\,TeV, $40.6$\,TeV at FCC-hh ($85$\,TeV, $20$\,ab$^{-1}$) and $48.2$\,TeV at $100$\,TeV ($30$\,ab$^{-1}$);
the discovery reach after a realistic selection is lower (Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}).

\section{Experimental design (first estimate)}\label{sec:design}
\emph{The numbers in this section use order-of-magnitude backgrounds and are superseded by Sec.~\ref{sec:sim}; the design logic stands.}
\emph{Selection.} Leading object $p_T>p_{\rm cut}$, ${\rm MET}>p_{\rm cut}$ and ${\rm MET}/S_T>0.8$, with $p_{\rm cut}$ optimised between $3$
and $16$\,TeV. \emph{Background.} $Z(\to\nu\bar\nu)+$jet with ${\rm MET}>p_{\rm cut}$, multiplied by $3$ for $W(\to\ell\nu)+$jet with a lost lepton
(order of magnitude). \emph{Statistics.} Discovery requires the Asimov significance \cite{CCGV11}
$Z=\sqrt{2[(s+b)\ln(1+s/b)-s]}=5$ and $s\geq5$. For $n=6$, $M_D=8$\,TeV the $5\sigma$ reach is $M_{\rm ext}\simeq33.5$\,TeV at FCC-hh
($85$\,TeV, $20$\,ab$^{-1}$) and $40.0$\,TeV at $100$\,TeV ($30$\,ab$^{-1}$); ten selected events (class and onset) are obtained up to
$35.8$ and $44.2$\,TeV, and, if $30\%$ of remnants are charged, ten heavy stable charged tracks up to $39.1$ and $46.3$\,TeV. Both towers
agree within $1$\,TeV. Most of the window is decided with very little luminosity ($7$\,pb$^{-1}$ at $M_{\rm ext}=11$\,TeV, $2$\,fb$^{-1}$
at $21$\,TeV). \emph{Detector.} Unprescaled ${\rm MET}$ and jet triggers at $1$\,TeV, calorimetry to $|\eta|\simeq6$ as in the FCC-hh
reference design \cite{FCC19}, $30$--$50$\,ps timing (a $\beta=0.39$ remnant arrives $52$\,ns late at $10$\,m) and $dE/dx$ ($\simeq7\times$
minimum ionising), giving $M=p/(\beta\gamma)$. \emph{Interpretation.} A KK excess without an onset up to the reach, an onset with balanced
high-multiplicity events, and an onset at $M_{\rm th}\gg M_D$ with invisible remnants respectively point to $c_l$ above the window, a
GR-like exterior (whose interior experiment cannot decide), and the model-U class, in which by the closure lemma the trapped region never
closes and the daughter persists (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}).

\section{Inner-horizon stability and a dynamical vacuum}\label{sec:inner}
\emph{Double-null evolution.} With $ds^2=-2e^{2\sigma}du\,dv+r^2d\Omega^2$ and a massless scalar, the tower's equations are
$h'(\psi)(r_{uu}-2\sigma_ur_u)=-r\phi_u^2$ (and $u\leftrightarrow v$), $r_{uv}=\tfrac12re^{2\sigma}[2\psi-(D-1)h/h']$, the wave equation, and a
$\sigma_{uv}$ equation obtained symbolically from constraint preservation (it reduces to GR for $h=\psi$ and gives $\sigma_{uv}=-ff''/8$ for the
static hole). Evolving the mass with the first-law fluxes keeps $\psi=h^{-1}(\mu/r^{D-1})<1/\alpha$. A scalar pulse into a $D=5$ Hayward hole
drives the Kretschmann scalar at the Cauchy horizon up by more than ten orders of magnitude by $v\simeq20\,l$ (converged), almost entirely in
the curvature of the radial-temporal plane, while $\psi$ stays below $0.95/\alpha$, $r$ stays at $r_-$ and the mass merely doubles; a later
infall feeds the same region. The curvature bound of the tower constrains the areal curvature, not the blueshifted product of influx and
outflux: the inner horizon of a perturbed model-U hole is a null curvature singularity of finite area (Ori-type; whether it is weak is
open), consistent with the general fragility of inner horizons and providing the continuous-flux analysis that \cite{DKS26} found missing.
\emph{Hysteresis.} In a bouncing closed interior with a scalar field in a false/true-vacuum potential, a field given $10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$ of the
bounce energy pumps successive cycles (maxima $1.5\to49$) until the interior enters the de Sitter branch: lasting inflation for false-vacuum
energies down to $0.05$ of the single-bounce threshold, by cosmological hysteresis \cite{ST12}; with no kick, or a zero-energy vacuum, the
interior cycles for ever. \emph{Causality.} The tower contains Gauss--Bonnet with coefficient $l^2$, so higher-spin states must appear at
$m\lesssim1/l=M_D/c_l$ \cite{CEMZ}: $c_l$ is tied to a second observable.

\section{Realistic simulation, reach and current data}\label{sec:sim}
\emph{Pipeline.} Backgrounds ($Z(\to\nu\bar\nu)$, $W(\to\ell\nu)$+jet, $t\bar t$, QCD) are generated with Pythia~8.3 \cite{Pythia83}; the
model-U production system is written to a Les Houches file and showered by Pythia, Hawking quanta being added in the hole frame; a
parametric detector follows the Delphes FCC-hh card (calorimetry to $|\eta|=6$), with a minimum-bias pile-up overlay ($\langle\mu\rangle=1000$,
charged removal $95\%$, neutral residual $30\%$). At $13$\,TeV the same chain with the exact ATLAS selection reproduces the published
background in all $13$ MET bins with one factor $K=1.18$ and a $10\%$ rms residual. \emph{Statistics.} A binned profile likelihood with
log-normal process normalisations, a MET-scale morphing, luminosity, a $30\%$ signal normalisation and per-bin MC statistics, fitted by
Fisher scoring, agrees with pseudo-experiments. \emph{Reach.} For $n=6$, $M_D^{\rm DL}=8$\,TeV the median $5\sigma$ discovery reach is
$M_{\rm ext}=33.4$\,TeV at $100$\,TeV ($30$\,ab$^{-1}$) and $26.8$\,TeV at $85$\,TeV ($20$\,ab$^{-1}$), the expected exclusion $35.0$ and
$28.0$\,TeV, insensitive to the background systematics: the remnant recoils only against initial-state radiation, so at the top of the
window the signal sits on the $Z(\nu\bar\nu)$ background at MET $\sim$ TeV. Discharge partons and spin-down quanta raise the acceptance.
\emph{What the signature establishes.} The exact $N$-event likelihood ratio gives a $5\sigma$ separation from a GR hole at the same threshold
with $12$--$13$ selected events at $S/B=1$, from ADD graviton emission with $\sim560$ and from vector-mediator dark matter with $240$--$370$;
the onset rate measures $c_l$ to $0.3$--$1\%$, and the rate--onset consistency with the KK-measured $(n,M_D)$ is what a dark-matter mediator
with a free coupling cannot reproduce. \emph{Remnants} are slow (median $\beta=0.13$; $+32$\,ns at $1.5$\,m, $+215$\,ns at $10$\,m), requiring
multi-crossing readout if charged.

\section{Limitations}
Rotating model-U solutions are unknown (rotation enters only as a signal variant); greybody factors are omitted; the detector is
parametric (Delphes-card level) and backgrounds are leading order with one validated $K$ factor; the double-null results are converged to
$v\simeq25\,l$ in $D=5$ only, and the strength of the Cauchy-horizon singularity is open; hysteresis is shown for a homogeneous interior;
the threshold window depends on the unknown coefficient $c_l$; and
the daughter itself is outside $J^-(\mathcal I^+)$, so experiments can test the interior class (onset, $T(M)$, remnants) but not the daughter
directly.
