# G11-31 — Research prospectus

**Question.** Can ordinary matter, collapsing in a well-defined gravitational theory, produce a spacetime region that persists
causally disconnected from its parent (a "daughter")? And what of such a theory could be tested in the laboratory?

## Where the project stands (Round 11, honest summary)

* **Theory.**
  * In a higher-dimensional theory with a regularising curvature tower (Model U), the static solution contains a daughter region.
  * Under realistic perturbations, the inner horizon becomes a weak null singularity in five dimensions. The classical equations then
    do not determine what lies beyond.
  * A fine-tuned family (inner-extremal) avoids this.
  * Ten dimensions is undecided.
  * **The generic daughter is therefore not established.** Establishing or refuting it is the main open problem.
* **Phenomenology.** The same theory predicts collider black holes with cold, invisible remnants. They are discoverable up to
  M_ext ≈ 33 TeV at a 100 TeV collider with a fully documented uncertainty budget. Such a discovery would test the theory, not the
  daughter.
* **Instrumentation.** The remnant signature needs 30–50 ps timing. A two-plane LGAD timing demonstrator is fully specified:
  requirements, sensors, front-end, DAQ, mechanics, BOM, test protocol and safety. It can be built with existing facilities.

## Why fund it

* **A: fundamental physics.** A tested numerical code and precise, falsifiable statements about inner horizons in pure-gravity regular
  black holes. This is a live topic (2024–2026 literature).
* **B: instrumentation.** A low-cost, staged timing demonstrator with standard deliverables (≤ 45 ps per plane, a beam-test TOF
  measurement). Useful for any slow heavy-particle search, independent of A.

## What it will not claim

That a daughter universe has been shown to form; that laboratory black holes are imminent (they need ~100 TeV machines); that any
cost has been quoted (all BOM prices are unresolved).
