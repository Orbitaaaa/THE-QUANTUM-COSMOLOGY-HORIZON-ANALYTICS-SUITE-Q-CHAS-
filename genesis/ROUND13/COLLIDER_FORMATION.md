# Round 13, priority 3: does a collider-formed hole host the same internal evolution?

**Answer: no.** Collider holes land in the right mass window, but they contain the wrong kind of matter.

## 1. What the collision provides

Collision data are two ultra-relativistic partons, i.e. Aichelburg–Sexl shock waves in D dimensions, at impact parameter b. A trapped
surface forms at the collision (Eardley–Giddings; Yoshino–Nambu). From the Round 6o generator at 100 TeV (n = 6, M_D(DL) = 8 TeV,
c_l = 0.70, conservative head-on trapping y = 0.588):

| Quantity | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| μ/μ_ext of produced holes | median 1.045; 80 % below 1.1; 97 % below 1.2 | collider holes are **near threshold**, the window where bound matter completes the full cycle (Dymnikova, D = 10) |
| Dimensionless spin a* | median ≈ 3.5 | strongly rotating (D = 10 Myers–Perry proxy): far from the spherical idealisation |
| Content inside the trapped surface | two massless partons plus gravitational radiation | all null |

## 2. Lemma 13-3 (unbound content does not bounce)

A fluid element with Γ > 1 (unbound) obeys U² = Γ² − f(r) in the static region. In the core, f ≤ 1, so U² > 0 everywhere: there is no
turning point and no bounce. Massless content is the limit Γ → ∞.

For a homogeneous unbound interior (open FRW, k = −1), ȧ² = a²ψ + 1 > 0, so a never turns round. Since ψ ≤ 1/α:
* a → 0 in finite proper time, with ȧ → 1;
* the density C/a^{D−1} diverges while the curvature stays bounded.

That is a matter caustic. Example: D = 10, C = 50, collapse from a = 5 reaches a → 0 at τ = 4.54 l, with ρ → 5×10⁵⁵ and ψα → 1. A
closed interior with the same tower and density bounces at a_b ≥ l instead (`FIGURES/round13.png`, panel f).

## 3. Consequence

**Proposition 13-4.**
* The matter that forms a collider hole is unbound and massless, so it does not bounce in the regular core.
* It streams through the core and approaches the inner horizon as v → ∞; this is Round 6o's outgoing shell, which reaches the Cauchy
  horizon with finite affine parameter.
* The region it encloses contains no comoving matter.

So collider-formed holes do **not** host the bound, cosmological daughter of Rounds 12–13. They host at most a light-like, empty
interior region that ends at the Cauchy horizon.

This sharpens the separation the project already made. A collider discovery would test the tower (Paper B), not the daughter (Paper C).

## 4. Non-sphericity, for bound collapse

**Effective estimate.** Shear behaves like a stiff component, σ² ∝ a^{−2(D−1)}, against dust ρ ∝ a^{−(D−1)}. A small anisotropy ε at
maximum expansion is therefore amplified by (a_max/a_b)^{D−1} at the bounce:

| Case | Amplification (a_max/a_b)^{D−1} |
|---|---|
| Dymnikova, μ = 1.05 μ_ext, χ_s = 1.4 (full-cycle case) | 2.5 |
| Hayward, μ = 1.2 μ_ext, χ_s = 1.0 | 106 |

Near-threshold cycles have small dynamic range, so small departures from sphericity survive them. Collision data, however, are O(1)
anisotropic (a* ~ 3.5) and unbound.

This is a GR scaling assumed to carry over: the anisotropic (Bianchi) sector of the tower is not known.

## 5. What would be needed

Bound, slowly infalling matter with Γ < 1, of total mass just above M_ext (about 34 TeV for the parameters above), collapsing from near
its own horizon. Colliders do not supply this.

Candidate channels that could, all hypothetical and not studied here:
* collapse of a dense, cold relic of this mass;
* early-universe (primordial) formation near the remnant scale.
