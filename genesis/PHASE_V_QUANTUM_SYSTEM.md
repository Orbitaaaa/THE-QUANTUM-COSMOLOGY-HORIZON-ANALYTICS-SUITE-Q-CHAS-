# Phase V — The quantum system: state space, channels, sectors, information, topology

**Exit condition (from the programme):** *a mathematically coherent quantum model with explicitly understood
limitations.* Status: **met for the reduced (minisuperspace) model.** The model is coherent on a precisely identified domain.
Its limitations are located exactly: where it stops, why, and what must be added there.

Main results:
1. **Theorem V-1 (channel structure).** The reduced quantum theory with the asymptotic clock is a scattering problem with two
   inequivalent ends. The parent channel ends at the black-hole horizon at $t_\infty=\infty$; it is a genuine asymptotic channel and needs no
   boundary condition. The daughter channel ends at the interior de Sitter horizon at **finite** $t_\infty$, where $P\to-\infty$; the reduced
   phase space has an edge there and a quantum boundary condition is needed. The daughter cannot be followed with the parent's clock:
   a unitary description must adjoin a daughter Hilbert space with its own clock.
2. **Theorem V-2 (sectors).** The daughter is not superselected in the reduced theory, since it is reached by tunnelling. It is
   **invisible to every exterior observable**: the exterior algebra is generated by $\hat H=M$, and the channel projector is not a function of it.
3. **Proposition V-3 (topology).** The tunnelling preserves spatial topology ($\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$) and creates an Einstein–Rosen throat. A
   baby-universe pinch-off ($\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3\sqcup S^3$) requires a singularity (Geroch, Tipler). It is not part of the tunnelling event.
4. **Information.** The which-branch entropy is $h(e^{-B})\approx Be^{-B}$, negligible next to $S_{BH}$. The real information question is the fate of Hawking
   partners that end up in the daughter. This model does not settle it.
5. **Semiclassical validity.** Excellent at GUT scales ($\hbar G|\theta'|\sim10^{-11}$), marginal at $10^{18}$ GeV ($\sim10^{-2}$). Stage One's "B = 133" samples
   have sub-Planckian masses, and their black holes are not semiclassical (correction to T6).

## 1. State space and constraint

Configuration variable: the wall area $u=R^2/2$ ($A=8\pi u$). Clock: the asymptotic Killing time $t_\infty$, with Hamiltonian $H=M$ (QUANTIZATION.md).
From Phase III:

* **constraint:** either the sector-resolved analytic constraint $Q_+\psi=0$, $Q_+=T-W_+$ (complex-angle convention), or the sector-blind
  squared constraint. They agree on double-flip transitions and differ on single-flip ones ($B_{\rm an}$ vs $B_{\rm sq}$);
* **measure:** Lebesgue $du$, the unique symmetric measure for the area-shift operator that grows more slowly than $e^{A/4\hbar G}$ (Lemma III-6);
* **physical states:** wave packets $\int dM\,c(M)\,\psi_M$ of solutions at fixed $M$, evolving as $e^{-iMt_\infty/\hbar}$.

## 2. Theorem V-1: where the reduced theory lives, and its two ends

The reduced Hamiltonian is a real function only on the static–static domain $f_{\rm in}(R)>0$, $f_{\rm out}(R)>0$ (Phase II). In regime A at fixed $M$:

| branch | static domain | edge | asymptotic time to edge | momentum at edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parent (bounded) | $2GM<R\le R_1$ | black-hole horizon $f_{\rm out}=0$ | $\infty$: $t_\infty\simeq-2GM\ln(R-2GM)$ | $P\to+\infty$ as $t_\infty\to\infty$ (log) |
| daughter (bounce) | $R_2\le R<1/\chi$ | interior de Sitter horizon $f_{\rm in}=0$ | **finite** | $P\to-\infty$ at finite $t_\infty$ (log) |

> **Theorem V-1.** (i) Along the parent branch $dt_\infty/d\tau=A_{\rm out}/f_{\rm out}$ diverges at $f_{\rm out}=0$ like $2GM/(R-2GM)$, so the horizon is reached
> only as $t_\infty\to\pm\infty$. The reduced flow is complete there: the edge is an asymptotic channel, in from the past horizon and out to the
> future horizon. (ii) Along the daughter branch the exterior stays static ($f_{\rm out}(R)>0$ for $R>R_2>2GM$), so $dt_\infty/d\tau$ is bounded and
> $f_{\rm in}=0$ is reached at finite $t_\infty$. There $\dot R/A_{\rm in}\to\pm1$ and $P=\frac RG[\operatorname{artanh}(\dot R/A_{\rm out})-\operatorname{artanh}(\dot R/A_{\rm in})]$ diverges
> logarithmically. The Hamiltonian flow on the reduced phase space is incomplete in finite time. Quantum mechanically, the reduced
> Hamiltonian is then not essentially self-adjoint on states supported up to the edge (classical incompleteness in finite time; WKB-level
> statement). A boundary condition at the edge is required: a reflecting self-adjoint extension, or an outgoing (absorbing) condition with a
> daughter Hilbert space adjoined.

**Numerical verification** (six masses, $\chi=\kappa=1$):
* the parent time grows by $2GM$ per e-fold of the distance to the horizon (measured slope / $2GM$ = 1.0000–1.0002);
* the daughter time converges to 0.0204, 0.0888, 0.1989, 0.4385, 0.6869, 1.5209 for $M=0.02\dots0.36$;
* $P_{\rm daughter}$ decreases by 2.30 per decade of the distance to the edge, i.e. $P\propto\ln(\text{distance})$.

**Consequences.**
* **Transition framework.** At fixed $M$ the parent is a scattering channel with one passage per laboratory bubble: it emerges from the past
  horizon and returns to the future horizon. In asymptotic time there are no repeated "attempts". The probability flux through the daughter
  edge is $e^{-B/\hbar}(1+O(\hbar))$, with $B=B_{\rm an}$ or $B_{\rm sq}$ (Phase III).
* **Physical choice at the daughter edge.** The edge is where the daughter's interior starts to inflate, causally beyond the exterior's reach.
  The outgoing condition is the physically appropriate one; a reflecting condition would send the daughter back into the parent, unphysically.
  Unitarity is restored on $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_{t_\infty}\oplus\mathcal H_{\rm dau}$, where $\mathcal H_{\rm dau}$ evolves with a daughter clock (wall proper time, or interior
  conformal time). **The exterior clock cannot serve as the daughter's time.** This is the precise, kinematic sense in which the daughter is a
  separate system after the transition.

## 3. Relational observables and Theorem V-2 (sectors)

Relational observables relative to the asymptotic clock, such as "the wall area when $t_\infty=t$", are defined on the static domain. For the
parent they exist for all $t$. For the daughter they exist only until the finite edge time $t_{\rm edge}(M)$.

> **Theorem V-2.** In the reduced theory every exterior observable is a function of $\hat H$, since the exterior is Schwarzschild($M$) by Birkhoff's
> theorem. Two states with the same mass distribution $|c(M)|^2$ give identical expectation values for all exterior observables. The projector
> $\Pi_{\rm dau}$ onto the daughter channel is not a function of $\hat H$, because stationary states at fixed $M$ contain both channels. Hence: (a) the daughter is
> **not** a superselection sector of the reduced theory, since it is connected to the parent by tunnelling; (b) it is **operationally superselected
> with respect to the exterior**: no exterior measurement detects it; (c) after the edge its degrees of freedom commute with all exterior
> observables, by causal disconnection (Stage One, Phase IV Cor. IV-2).

This is the reduced-model version of the question posed for the crossed-product construction (NEW_APPROACHES N2). The exterior algebra here is
abelian (functions of $M$). A von Neumann-algebraic treatment with matter fields is open (P-Q5).

## 4. Proposition V-3: topology change

> The time-symmetric slices through the turning points have the following spatial topology (`causal.time_symmetric_slice`):
> * **parent** at $R_1$: $\varepsilon_{\rm in}=\varepsilon_{\rm out}=+1$, no extremal sphere, topology $\mathbb R^3$;
> * **daughter** at $R_2$ ($\varepsilon_{\rm out}=-1$): a maximal sphere at the wall and a **minimal sphere at the Einstein–Rosen bridge** $r=2GM$, plus the de Sitter
>   equator if $\varepsilon_{\rm in}=-1$. Topology $\mathbb R^3$ (interior ball ∪ region III ∪ bridge ∪ region I).
>
> So the quantum transition **preserves the spatial topology and changes the geometry**: it creates a throat. It is a transition in the sign of
> the exterior extrinsic curvature, the sector of Phase III. The separation into $\mathbb R^3\sqcup S^3$, a detached closed daughter, cannot happen smoothly.
> Geroch's theorem (J. Math. Phys. 8, 782 (1967)) excludes topology change across a compact region without closed timelike curves.
> Tipler's (1977) extension with energy conditions requires singularities. In our solutions the pinch-off can happen only at the black-hole
> singularity or the evaporation endpoint, outside the semiclassical model.

Checked for six masses: every parent slice has no extremal sphere, and every daughter slice has the wall maximum plus the bridge minimum.
These references are cited from standard knowledge and were not re-read in this session.

## 5. Information

* **Which-branch entropy.** With $p=e^{-B}$, $S_{\rm ch}=h(p)\approx p(1+B)$ nats. For the GUT samples with semiclassical black holes ($M>10\,m_{\rm Pl}$),
  $\log_{10}p$ lies between $-3.05\times10^4$ and $-2.84\times10^6$, against $S_{BH}=4\pi GM^2/\hbar$ from $8.3\times10^5$ to $6.7\times10^{11}$. The branch information is utterly
  negligible.
* **The real issue.** If the exterior black hole evaporates, the partners of the Hawking quanta are inside the horizon. In the daughter case
  part of that interior is the inflating region, which stays causally disconnected. In semiclassical bookkeeping the exterior final state is
  then mixed, with up to $S_{BH}$ of entanglement carried off by the daughter. This is the baby-universe information-loss scenario (Hawking;
  Coleman's α-parameters). Deciding between loss, α-parameters and recovery needs quantum fields on these backgrounds and a non-perturbative
  inner product. That is beyond this reduced model (P-Q6).
* **Phase III link.** Horizon-winding sectors carry weights $e^{\pm A/4G}$ (Theorem III-1): horizon-entropy factors. A thermal (Hartle–Hawking-like)
  exterior would weight them; the pure laboratory state does not.

## 6. Validity of the semiclassical approximation

* **WKB parameter of the area-shift constraint**, $\varepsilon=\hbar G|d\theta/du|$ on the barrier, Planck units, turning-point neighbourhoods excluded:
  * vacuum = wall scale = $10^{16}$ GeV: $\varepsilon=2\times10^{-11}$–$3\times10^{-10}$ over all masses, barrier areas $10^{8}$–$10^{12}\,\ell_P^2$: **excellent**;
  * $10^{18}$ GeV: $\varepsilon\approx(4\text{–}29)\times10^{-3}$, areas about $10^4\,\ell_P^2$: **marginal**; $O(\varepsilon)$ corrections to $B$ of a few per mille.
* **$B\gg1$** in all samples (smallest: 105 at $10^{18}$ GeV).
* **Correction to Stage One T6.** The samples with $M/M_{\rm cr}=10^{-9},10^{-6}$ ($M=5.6\times10^{-12}$ and $5.6\times10^{-9}$ kg, $B=135$ and 793) have $M<m_{\rm Pl}=2.2\times10^{-8}$ kg.
  The tunnelling barrier itself is semiclassical: $R_{1,2}\sim10^2$–$10^3\,\ell_P$ and $\varepsilon\sim10^{-11}$. But the black hole the parent would form is sub-Planckian
  ($2GM\ll\ell_P$), so the subsequent parent evolution is not semiclassical. For semiclassical black holes, $M\gtrsim10\,m_{\rm Pl}$, the GUT exponent is
  $B\gtrsim7\times10^4$. TUNNELLING.md T6 and RESULTS R11 are annotated accordingly.
* **Evaporation competition.** Suppose a black-hole → white-hole bounce at $r=0$ were added, allowing repeated attempts. That is outside this
  model: in asymptotic time the parent makes one passage. At most $N=t_{\rm ev}/(2GM)=2560\pi(M/m_{\rm Pl})^2$ attempts fit before evaporation (single
  massless species). The total daughter probability is then bounded by $N e^{-B}\le10^{-184}$ over all semiclassical-black-hole samples, and by
  $10^{-30490}$ at the GUT scale.

## 7. Limitations (explicit)

1. **Reduced theory.** One degree of freedom, the wall radius; no matter fields, no gravitons. Constraints are solved classically. The
   Dirac/Wheeler–DeWitt quantization of the full system could differ beyond WKB.
2. **Single clock.** The asymptotic clock covers the static domain only (Theorem V-1). The daughter edge condition and the daughter clock
   are additional input.
3. **Exponent only.** Prefactors are not computed. The choice between $B_{\rm an}$ and $B_{\rm sq}$ in the single-flip class remains open (P-Q9).
4. **Singularities.** The behaviour at $R=0$ inside the black hole (a bounce or not) is undetermined. It affects only the attempt count, which
   matters only if a bounce is postulated.
5. **Information.** Settling it requires quantum fields (Hawking partners) and an algebraic treatment (crossed products, P-Q5).
