# Round 12 — Getting the finite daughter cycle to work

**Goal (from the request).** A daughter region forms, expands, then recollapses or ends. It may be finite and need not expand much. It
must actually form and do something.

**Answer: the cycle works, inside Model U as it stands, with no change to the theory.**

The loophole is that **the daughter does not have to cross the Cauchy horizon.** Inside the black hole, the bounced matter forms a closed,
expanding piece of universe. That region lies in the *maximal globally hyperbolic development*: the part of spacetime the field
equations determine uniquely from the initial data. The Cauchy horizon, which Round 11 found singular, only bounds this region from the
future. Within it:

| Case | Forms | Expands | Reaches maximum | Recollapses | Second bounce | Then |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: D = 5, μ = 20 μ_ext, χ_s = 0.5 | yes | yes (×10.7 in radius, 1.8 e-folds of acceleration) | — | — | — | predictable history ends while still expanding |
| B: D = 10 Hayward, μ = 1.2 μ_ext, χ_s = 1.0 | yes | yes | **yes** | **yes**, to 87 % of a_max | — | ends during the recollapse |
| C: D = 10 Dymnikova, μ = 1.05 μ_ext, χ_s = 1.4 | yes | yes | **yes** | **yes** | **yes** | ends just after the second bounce |

* Everything in the table is determined by the field equations. No continuation through a singularity is used.
* The whole cycle happens behind the parent's event horizon.
* It holds on open sets of initial data (it is not fine-tuned).

## 1. What counts as a finite daughter (definitions used)

1. **D1, formed:** starts from regular, untrapped initial data in an asymptotically flat parent.
2. **D2, bounce:** matter contracts, bounces at a scale a_b ≥ l and re-expands.
3. **D3, cosmological:** the region is a homogeneous, closed FRW patch with Hubble flow, here with an accelerating phase.
4. **D4, recollapse:** it reaches a maximum and contracts. A *full cycle* means it reaches a second bounce.
5. **D5, hidden:** it lies outside J⁻(I⁺) of the parent (behind the event horizon).
6. **D6, determined:** it lies in the maximal globally hyperbolic development, so no Cauchy-horizon continuation is needed.
7. **D7, generic:** the result holds on an open set of initial data.
8. **D8, robust:** generic perturbations leave it unaffected, except in an arbitrarily thin layer at its future boundary.

A causally *permanent*, infinite daughter is not required by the request and is not claimed.

## 2. The construction: the Cauchy-development daughter

**Set-up.**
* A homogeneous dust ball of comoving radius χ_s starts from rest in the parent.
* Its interior is a closed FRW patch: ȧ² = a²ψ(a) − 1, with ψ = h⁻¹(C/a^{D−1}) and C = μ/sin^{D−1}χ_s.
* Outside is the static model-U hole of mass μ > μ_ext.
* Initial data are untrapped: R₀ = a_max sin χ_s > r₊.

**Lemma 12-0 (no turning point in the core).** f decreases strictly on (0, r₋].
* *Hayward, analytic:* f′ = 2μr((D−3)r^{D−1}/2 − μ)/(r^{D−1}+μ)² < 0 for r^{D−1} < 2μ/(D−3), and r₋ lies below the minimum of f.
* *Dymnikova:* checked numerically.

So the surface (ṙ² = E² − f) cannot turn round inside the core. After the bounce it must leave the core through r = r₋ at an event E.

**Theorem 12-1 (Cauchy-development daughter).** Let

  D = { post-bounce events of the dust interior with χ < χ_s − (η − η_E) }  (η = conformal time of the FRW patch).

Then D lies in the maximal globally hyperbolic development of the initial data, and outside J⁻(I⁺) of the parent.

*Proof.*
1. In ingoing Eddington coordinates the core's future boundary r = r₋ is reached by outgoing curves only as v → ∞. So E lies on the
   exterior Cauchy horizon N1 = {v = ∞}, and every later point of the surface lies beyond it.
2. Inside the dust, the boundary of the future domain of dependence is generated by the ingoing null rays from E, i.e.
   χ = χ_s − (η − η_E). A point beyond this cone has a past causal curve leaving through the surface after E. That curve continues
   into the region beyond N1 and down to timelike infinity without meeting the initial slice. A point before the cone has its whole
   past inside the dust interior or before E.
3. All post-bounce events lie to the future of the surface's crossing of r₊, so they are inside the event horizon. ∎

**Proposition 12-2 (the de Sitter timing law).**
* Just after the bounce the core is de Sitter-like (ψ → 1/α, a ≈ √α cosh(τ/√α)), and r₋ → √α for μ ≫ μ_ext.
* The surface reaches r₋ when a_E sin χ_s = r₋, i.e. cos(η_E − η_b) = sin χ_s. So η_cone − η_b = (η_E − η_b) + χ_s = π/2.
* The Cauchy-horizon cone reaches the daughter's centre exactly when the core's de Sitter phase would end. **The Cauchy horizon is the
  core's de Sitter horizon.**
* Numerically, η_cone − η_b lies in [π/2, 2.00] over the scan, and equals π/2 to 10⁻⁷ at μ = 10⁴ μ_ext.
* An ODE solver and an independent quadrature agree to 10⁻⁵.

**Proposition-N 12-3 (expansion, D = 5, χ_s = 0.5).**

| μ / μ_ext | Centre's growth before the cone | Accelerating e-folds |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | ×9.0 | 1.66 |
| 100 | ×16.0 | 2.25 |
| 10⁴ | ×50.9 | 3.43 |

* The centre's final radius follows a_cone ≈ 1.72 C^{1/(D−1)} at all three masses.
* The accelerating e-folds follow N ≈ ln C/(D−1), as in Round 6l.
* For large holes the daughter therefore *inflates* inside the determined region. It ends while still expanding, at about
  1.7× the size where inflation stops.

**Proposition-N 12-4 (the full cycle).** Recollapse inside D requires the conformal half-period of the daughter to be shorter than
the cone budget (≥ π/2). That happens for compact balls near threshold in higher dimensions. On a grid in μ/μ_ext ∈ [1.005, 30] and
χ_s ∈ [0.3, 1.565], counting initially untrapped balls only:

| Tower, D | Maximum + recollapse inside D | Full cycle (second bounce) inside D |
|---|---|---|
| Dymnikova, 10 | **85 %** of the grid | **2.8 %** (μ ≲ 1.1 μ_ext, χ_s ≳ 1.27) |
| Hayward, 10 | 79 % | 0 |
| Hayward, 8 | 54 % | 0 |
| Hayward, 5 | 0.4 % | 0 |

The coarse scan over D = 5 … 10 (both towers) shows the recollapse fraction rising steadily with D.

* Hayward's conformal half-period never drops below 1.19 (D = 10), 1.11 (D = 11) or 1.05 (D = 12), even near criticality. So with dust,
  Hayward towers reach the maximum and recollapse partway, but the second bounce falls just outside D.
* The Dymnikova tower's half-period drops to 0.80, so the full cycle fits.

## 3. Robustness and limits

* **Perturbations.** Mass inflation grows only as v → ∞ (Round 11). A point inside D receives influx only from v below some finite
  v_max for that point, so the blueshift there is bounded. D minus an arbitrarily thin layer at its future boundary is stable at the
  level of the Ori model (Proposition). The singular Cauchy horizon is where the daughter's *predictable* history ends. Its matter
  reaches it with finite distortion (Tipler-weak in D = 5, Round 11).
* **Not claimed:** what happens beyond the Cauchy horizon; a permanent or infinite daughter.
* **Realism:**
  * The construction uses dust balls released from rest. The recollapse regime needs compact balls (χ_s ≳ 0.45–0.85 in D = 10, i.e. starting
    within a factor of order one of their horizon) and near-threshold masses in D ≥ 7.
  * Collider holes are near threshold and D = 10. But they form from colliding partons, not dust balls; whether they host a dust-like
    daughter is open (P-R12-b).
  * Pressure: every perfect fluid bounces (Round 6n). Stiffer matter shortens the conformal cycle, which helps. A full hydrodynamic
    check of the timing is P-R12-a.
* **EFT and quantum corrections.** The near-threshold daughter lives at ψα ≈ 0.5–1, where the tower is outside any EFT truncation
  (Round 11, G11-05). Semiclassical back-reaction inside D has not been computed.

## 5. What changed relative to Round 11

* **Round 11 asked** whether the daughter can be *continued through* the Cauchy horizon. For generic data the answer was no (D = 5).
* **Round 12 asks** whether the daughter needs to reach it at all. For the finite daughter the answer is no: its formation, inflation,
  maximum and (in D ≥ 7, near threshold) recollapse all come first. The singular Cauchy horizon is where the predictable history
  *ends*, which is the "ends" branch of the requested cycle.
* **Paper C.** Its gate ("no main claim resting on a measure-zero family") is now passed: the recollapse regime is an open set.
