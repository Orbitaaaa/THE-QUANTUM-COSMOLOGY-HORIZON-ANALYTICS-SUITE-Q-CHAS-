# Round 6: the search for a controllable creation pathway (GENESIS MECHANISM ATLAS)

**Programme (Roadmap from Round 4 to the Machine, Round 6).** Find a physical mechanism that could produce a daughter universe in a
controllable way. The tasks 6A–6G were offered as guidance. This round instead starts from a complete map of the
assumptions that any route must break, so that the search is exhaustive rather than a list of candidates. Every route is then
priced by what breaking its assumption costs.

Everything below is theory. Where a later round would need an experiment, the experiment is named only as a test of physics
(Round 8). **No apparatus is designed and no experimental procedure is proposed.**

Labels:

* **[Theorem]**: proved under stated hypotheses;
* **[Proposition-N]**: numerical;
* **[Proposition]**: derived at the stated level of rigour (scaling, O(1) constants);
* **[Literature]**: a result of the cited work, not re-derived here;
* **[Conjecture]**;
* **[Assessment]**: a judgement, flagged as such.

## The answer to Problem B ("is there a more accessible formation mechanism?")

**Within established physics: no, and that is now a closure result, not a failure to find one.** Five results (G1, G2, S7, Wall's GSL theorem,
strong cosmic censorship) leave every route either Planck-limited, astronomically massive, unpredictable, or exponentially suppressed. The best
known-physics options are:
* (a) tunnelling at the frontier, $P\le10^{-23}$ per prepared bubble at $3\times10^{17}$ GeV;
* (b) the same with a stabilised seed, which would convert in $\sim0.1$ s, but needs a Planck-density seed and a stabiliser that has not been found;
* (c) a charged or rotating collapse, which crosses a Cauchy horizon classically but needs $M\ge0.16\,m_P^3/\sqrt\rho$ (5 g at GUT density) and
  has an outcome the initial data do not fix.

**With one discovery: yes, enormously more accessible.** In a field sector with stable, self-amplifying NEC violation (Route G), formation is
classical and deterministic: $P\approx1$ per seed, with laboratory-scale seed sizes and stresses and a small ADM mass. This holds provided seeds whose edge
opens a throat exist, which is the first calculation needed (P-R6b). The required stress is
generated by the sector's own dynamics. The price is explicit:
* the sector must violate the GSL and S-matrix positivity;
* its seeds must be built so that the throat opens inside a NEC-violating boundary layer (Proposition G7). A seed with a sharp edge in vacuum
  becomes a visible bubble that consumes the parent.

The sector's existence is testable without making a universe (Part C5). This is the most accessible mechanism that exists in any consistent
theory we could find.

## Summary: the answer of this round

1. **The search space is closed by five results.** Every route to a hidden daughter that starts in an asymptotically flat parent must defeat
   one of them:
   * **G1** (classical budget, new) [Theorem];
   * **G2** (no bridge crossing for thin walls of any matter, new) [Theorem];
   * the semiclassical tunnelling frontier S7 (Round 2) [Theorem given S3];
   * Wall's GSL singularity theorem [Literature];
   * strong cosmic censorship, for routes through Cauchy horizons [Literature].

   So a route must give up one of: (i) the null energy condition (NEC) *together with* the generalised second law (GSL), (ii) predictability
   (it passes a Cauchy horizon), (iii) the semiclassical regime (Planck densities), or (iv) the laboratory setting (a cosmological parent).
   There is no fifth option.
2. **The classical budget (Theorem G1).** An anti-trapped sphere forms from laboratory data only if, along some ingoing null ray,
   $$\frac{2Gm}{r}\Big|_0+8\pi G\!\int r\,(-T_{uu})\,\frac{du}{\alpha^2}\ \ge\ 1 .$$
   Either the initial data are already near their own Schwarzschild radius, or a NEC-violating "null force" of order
   $c^4/8\pi G=4.8\times10^{42}$ N acts. This is $1/2\pi$ of the conjectured maximum force. The requirement does not depend on size: a 1 m
   region needs NEC-violating stresses of $5\times10^{42}$ Pa, i.e. energy-density scale 22 GeV. Casimir-type quantum NEC violation falls
   short by at least 50 orders of magnitude at every size above the Planck length.
3. **Thin walls cannot do it (Theorem G2).** At a flip of the exterior orientation the wall potential equals $f_{\rm out}$. A classical flip therefore
   happens inside a horizon, for **any** surface matter, NEC-violating or not. With growing radius that is a white hole, whose past is a
   singularity (Schwarzschild), a Cauchy horizon (Reissner–Nordström) or a cosmological region (SdS). Exotic matter confined to a wall gives
   at most a *visible* inflating bubble. That needs negative asymptotic tension, and such walls make Minkowski space decay upward with the same exponent as
   the CdL bounce (Proposition G3). A visible bubble also grows into the parent universe. It is excluded both as a goal and by our survival
   ($B\gtrsim560$).
4. **Routes without exotic matter are Planck-limited or astronomical.**
   * Tunnelling: $P\le10^{-23}$ per prepared bubble at $E_{\rm vac}\approx3\times10^{17}$ GeV, $R\approx100\,\ell_P$.
   * Stabilising the seed changes the picture (Proposition G5). With attempt frequency $\omega\sim c/R\sim10^{41}$ Hz, a seed at the frontier
     converts in $\sim0.1$ s at $R=300\,\ell_P$. **Suppression is then not the bottleneck; preparing a stable Planck-density seed is.**
   * A charged exterior supplies a classical route through the inner (Cauchy) horizon (Sakai et al.; Proposition G4). It needs the prepared
     object to exceed $M_{\rm cr}\approx0.16\,m_P^3/\sqrt\rho$: about 5 g at GUT density, about an Earth mass at TeV density. Its outcome is
     not determined by the initial data.
5. **The only class with laboratory-scale requirements is self-amplifying stable NEC violation** ("genesis seeds", Route G). In Galileon-type
   theories with kinetic braiding the NEC-violating stress grows on its own, $|p|\propto t^{-4}$. A seed of any size $L$, prepared at modest
   stress and with small ADM mass, evolves deterministically until the budget G1 is met and anti-trapped spheres form. In the
   Galilean-genesis scaling, a 1 cm seed started at $10^{11}$ Pa forms its daughter after $\approx4$ ms, with the budget exceeded 129-fold.
   **This is the strongest candidate found.** It is controllable and has $P\approx1$ per correctly prepared seed. **Its price is precise:** the
   sector must violate the black-hole area theorem and hence the GSL (Wall's theorem forbids "restarting inflation" otherwise). It must also
   violate S-matrix positivity, and generically propagate superluminally around backgrounds. In the simplest single-field model, a laboratory
   seed cannot even be interpolated to the Minkowski vacuum without strong coupling (Rubakov 2013).
   **The seed's edge decides between daughter and catastrophe (Proposition G7, new).** If the NEC-violating region ends at a thin interface in
   vacuum, the interface keeps its exterior orientation (G2). Its energy must then turn negative at $H^2R^2=2GM/R$, before the interior
   anti-traps, and the seed becomes a visible inflating bubble that grows into the parent. A hidden daughter requires the throat to open inside
   a NEC-violating boundary layer that itself meets the budget G1.
6. **What would have to be discovered.** A field sector with stable, self-amplifying NEC violation, coupled weakly enough to ordinary matter
   to be safe and strongly enough to be prepared. Its existence can be tested **without making a universe**, through positivity violation in
   its scattering, superluminal propagation, or classical violation of the area theorem. Without such a sector there is no laboratory route
   within known physics. With it, the route is classical.
7. **Safety, as a theoretical constraint (Round 9 input).** A hidden daughter in an asymptotically flat parent always leaves an exterior
   black hole of mass $M_{\rm ADM}$ (Birkhoff). For a semiclassical throat $M_{\rm ADM}\gg m_P$, so its evaporation releases at least
   $m_Pc^2\approx2$ GJ. That is about 0.5 t TNT at $M=m_P$ and 5 t at $10\,m_P$, released in $<10^{-35}$ s. Visible bubbles would engulf the
   parent and are excluded outright.

## Part A — The closure theorems

### A1. Theorem G1 (the classical budget) — [Theorem]
*Spherical symmetry, any matter. Metric $ds^2=-\alpha^2du\,dv+r^2d\Omega^2$, with $u,v$ increasing to the future and $v$ outward.
Laboratory data are a regular centre and an initial outgoing cone with no trapped, marginally trapped or anti-trapped sphere. An
anti-trapped sphere ($r_u,r_v>0$) can then occur at $(u_1,v)$ only if*
$$16\pi G\,r_v(u_0,v)\int_{u_0}^{u_1}r\,(-T_{uu})\,\frac{du}{\alpha^2}\ \ge\ 1-\frac{2Gm(u_0,v)}{r(u_0,v)},$$
*where $(u_0,v)$ is the ray's point on the initial cone. With $v$ normalised so that $r_v=\frac12$ there (the flat-space normalisation),
this reads $8\pi G\int r(-T_{uu})du/\alpha^2\ge1-2Gm/r|_0$.*

*Proof.*
1. The $E_{uu}$ constraint is $\alpha^2\partial_u(r_u/\alpha^2)=-4\pi G\,rT_{uu}$ (Prop. IV-1), and $r_ur_v/\alpha^2=-\frac14(1-2Gm/r)$ by the
   definition of the Misner–Sharp mass.
2. Integrate along the ingoing ray: $r_u/\alpha^2|_{u_1}=-\frac{1-2Gm/r}{4r_v}\big|_{u_0}+4\pi G\int r(-T_{uu})du/\alpha^2$.
3. Anti-trapping requires the left-hand side to be positive.
4. Both sides transform identically under $u\to\tilde u(u)$ and $v\to\tilde v(v)$. $\square$

**Reading.** $c^4/8\pi G=4.815\times10^{42}$ N is a force, so the budget is scale-free. For a region of size $L$ ($r\sim\Delta u\sim L$) the
required NEC-violating stress is $\sim(1-C_0)c^4/8\pi GL^2$, where $C_0=2Gm/r|_0$ is the compactness:

| $L$ | required $\lvert T_{uu}\rvert$ | energy-density scale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 m | $4.8\times10^{42}$ Pa | 22 GeV |
| 1 cm | $4.8\times10^{46}$ Pa | 220 GeV |
| 1 μm | $4.8\times10^{54}$ Pa | 22 TeV |
| 1 fm | $4.8\times10^{72}$ Pa | $7\times10^8$ GeV |
| $100\,\ell_P$ | $1.8\times10^{108}$ Pa | $5\times10^{17}$ GeV |

* Casimir energy between plates at 10 nm gives a null force of $4\times10^{-12}$ N, which is 54 orders short (`casimir_shortfall`).
* Quantum NEC violation obeying quantum energy inequalities scales as $L^{-4}$ against the budget's $L^{-2}$. The two meet only at
  $L\sim\ell_P$ (figure, panel 1), which reproduces the species bound $N\gtrsim\frac83S_{\rm dS}$ of the previous round.
* The alternative, $C_0\to1$, means compressing the matter to its own Schwarzschild radius. That costs positive energy
  $c^4r/2G$, with the same Planck force times the size.

**What G1 does not say.** It is necessary, not sufficient. A large but incoherent NEC violation need not anti-trap anything: a ghost-sign scalar
pulse of Planckian amplitude did not anti-trap any sphere in the Phase IV solver (F19). The violation must be organised, so that the ingoing
rays through one sphere all collect it.

### A2. Theorem G2 (no bridge crossing for thin walls of any matter) and Proposition G3 (visible bubbles) — [Theorem]
**Theorem G2.** *Consider a thin wall with arbitrary surface energy $\sigma(R)$ (stress fixed by conservation) between interior de Sitter and an
exterior $f_{\rm out}$ that is Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström or Schwarzschild–de Sitter. Wherever $A_{\rm out}=0$, the effective potential is
$V=f_{\rm out}$. A classical flip of the exterior orientation therefore occurs only where $f_{\rm out}\le0$, inside a horizon. With $\dot R>0$
this is a white-hole region, whose past is:*
* *Schwarzschild: the past singularity;*
* *Reissner–Nordström: the inner horizon of the previous universe, a Cauchy horizon;*
* *Schwarzschild–de Sitter: the expanding cosmological region of a horizon-scale parent.*

*Proof.* $V=f_{\rm out}-A_{\rm out}^2$, and $\dot R^2=-V$. In the future interior $\dot R<0$. $\square$
(`GeneralShell.bridge_crossing_report`: the identity holds to $10^{-9}$ for three surface-energy laws, including a wall-plus-negative-dust law
of the Guendelman–Sakai type.)

**Consequence.** On a wall, the NEC requires $\sigma\ge0$ and $\sigma+p=-R\sigma'/2\ge0$. Violating it on the wall alone cannot produce a
*hidden* daughter. The Farhi–Guth conclusion for thin walls does not depend on the wall's energy conditions at all; it depends on the exterior
being vacuum. To open a bridge classically, the NEC must be violated **in the bulk**, which is where G1 applies.

**Proposition G3 (visible bubbles) — [Theorem for the orientation; Proposition for the rate].**
* At large $R$, $A_{\rm out}\simeq-(\chi^2+\kappa_\infty^2)R/2\kappa_\infty$. A bubble that stays on our side of every horizon and inflates
  must therefore have **negative asymptotic tension**, $\kappa_\infty<0$.
* At $M=0$ the canonical exponent depends only on $\kappa^2$. So a negative-tension wall nucleates a visible de Sitter bubble in Minkowski space
  with exactly the positive-tension CdL exponent: 0.0214, 0.785 and 0.534 for $\kappa/\chi=0.3$, 1 and 3, against $B(0)$ from `tunnelling`.
* Our past light cone contains about $H_0^{-4}\sim10^{243}$ Planck four-volumes. If such walls existed, survival would require $B\gtrsim560$.
* A visible bubble's wall expands outward forever and converts the parent to the false vacuum (the "eats up the original universe" outcome
  of Guendelman–Sakai 2008). **It is not a daughter but the destruction of the parent. It is excluded as a goal.**

### A3. The GSL closes the bulk-NEC route unless the sector violates it — [Literature + Proposition]
* **Wall (2010/13, arXiv:1010.5513) [Literature].** If the GSL holds, "it is impossible to form baby universes which eventually become
  independent of the mother universe, or to restart inflation". Laboratory creation is restarting inflation. The theorem is semiclassical
  and robust to higher-curvature terms, and it does not cover tunnelling (a non-classical geometry).
* **Proposition (classical area-theorem violation).** For minimally coupled matter, Hawking's area theorem rests on the NEC. A stable classical
  NEC-violating flux through a horizon lowers its area with no compensating entropy outside, which violates the GSL at the classical level. So a
  sector that can restart inflation classically must violate the GSL. This agrees with Wall's theorem read in reverse.
* **The non-minimal-coupling loophole is closed.**
  * In scalar–tensor theories with $F(\phi)R$, the Jordan frame shows an effective NEC violation with no ghost.
  * But causal independence (horizons, disconnection, the Penrose diagram) is conformally invariant. In the Einstein frame the scalar is
    healthy and minimally coupled, so Wall's theorem applies with the Wald entropy $FA/4G$.
  * A Jordan-frame-only "inflation" that remains causally connected needs $F\to0$, i.e. $G_{\rm eff}\to\infty$: strong coupling.
* Beyond-Horndeski theories, which are not conformally equivalent to Einstein gravity plus a healthy scalar, remain the open case. They are Route G.

### A4. The tunnelling frontier and the role of stabilisation — [Theorem (S7) + Proposition G5]
Corollary S7 bounds any thin-barrier transition: $B\ge1.671\sqrt{B_{\rm CdL}(M/m_P)(R/\ell_P)}\,(1-0.73\kappa^2/\chi^2)$.

* **An unstabilised laboratory bubble gets one attempt** (D5): it collapses into a black hole after one bounce, so $P\le e^{-53}$.
* **A seed that is stabilised against collapse attempts once per oscillation,** so $\tau=e^B/\omega$ (`frontier_seed_window`):

| $R/\ell_P$ | 100 | 200 | 300 | 350 | 400 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $B$ at the frontier | 52.8 | 74.7 | 91.5 | 98.9 | 105.7 | 167.1 |
| lifetime | $5\times10^{-19}$ s | 3 ns | 0.09 s | 160 s | 2 days | $2\times10^{32}$ s |

**Stabilisation turns the $10^{-23}$ suppression into a millisecond-to-second lifetime.** This is the one place where the per-bubble probability
was the wrong figure of merit. But a stabiliser compatible with a bridge is not available:
* A charge-stabilised super-extremal object has no horizon, hence no daughter branch (G4).
* Sub-extremal stable orbits outside $r_+$ were not found in our scans.
* A coarse scan of wall-plus-dust two-component walls found no breathing orbit either (Guendelman–Sakai report such orbits elsewhere in
  parameter space).
* Every stabilised seed would still have Planck density ($E_{\rm vac}\sim10^{17}$ GeV, $R\sim300\,\ell_P$).

### A5. The Cauchy-horizon class (Proposition G4) — [Proposition-N + Literature]
Take a Reissner–Nordström exterior (electric or magnetic charge $Q<M$) and interior de Sitter (`ChargedBubble`).
* **The laboratory exponent barely moves.** $B$ changes by less than 3% for $Q\le0.99M$ (κ = χ, M = 0.1: 0.9093 against 0.9095 at Q = 0).
  Charge does not help tunnelling.
* **Charge supplies a bounce.** Inside $r_-$, at $R_{\rm bounce}\simeq Q^2/2M$, the effective potential has a thin barrier.
* **Above the barrier-free mass the route is classical.** A bubble collapsing from our region I then crosses $r_+$ and $r_-$, bounces, and
  re-expands through the next universe's white-hole region. There it flips its exterior orientation between the horizons, and it expands forever
  with an inflating interior. This is the route of Sakai, Nakao, Ishihara & Kobayashi (2006) and Borde–Trodden–Vachaspati (1998).
  * The threshold is continuous with the uncharged one: $M_{\rm cr}=0.385,0.386,0.411,0.445$ for $Q/M=0.01,0.1,0.5,0.7$ at $\kappa=\chi$.
  * It rises with charge ($0.486\to0.674$ at $\kappa=0.3\chi$). The route closes when $r_->1/\sqrt{\chi^2+\kappa^2}$.
* **Physical threshold.** $M_{\rm cr}\approx0.45/(G\chi)=0.16\,m_P^3/\sqrt\rho$, the black hole as large as the false vacuum's Hubble radius:

| $\rho^{1/4}$ | $1.2\times10^{19}$ GeV | $10^{17}$ GeV | $10^{16}$ GeV | $10^{10}$ GeV | 1 TeV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $M_{\rm cr}$ | $3\times10^{-9}$ kg | $5\times10^{-5}$ kg | 5 g | $5\times10^{9}$ kg | $5\times10^{23}$ kg |

* **Predictability fails at the Cauchy horizon [Literature].**
  * Generic perturbations turn $r_-$ into a mass-inflation singularity: $C^0$-extendible, but generically not $C^1$ (Dafermos 2003).
  * Burko and Ori showed it is weak, so extended objects can cross it.
  * The continuation beyond is not fixed by the initial data, so the outcome is not under laboratory control.
  * The Kerr analogue (angular momentum instead of charge) has the same structure. It is not computed here (it needs 3+1).

## Part C — Route G in detail: genesis seeds

### C1. Why this is the only laboratory-scale class
G1 fixes the null force that must act along the ingoing rays through the sphere that becomes anti-trapped. Laboratory sources cannot supply
$c^4/8\pi G$ against a material, and quantum NEC violation is QEI-limited. The input requirement disappears only if the medium *generates* the
NEC-violating stress itself: a phase whose $|\rho+p|$ grows under its own dynamics, while the ADM mass stays fixed by the initial configuration
(the growth is paid for by gravitational binding). Known examples are the Galilean genesis phase and its relatives:
* Creminelli–Nicolis–Trincherini 2010, and the subluminal variant of 2012;
* Elder–Joyce–Khoury 2013, an interpolating, subluminal and weakly coupled solution with dilatation broken explicitly;
* Pirtskhalava–Santoni–Trincherini–Uttayarat 2014, "inflation from Minkowski space".

### C2. A concrete field theory (Round 5 input)
The NEC-violating phase of the Galilean genesis Lagrangian (mostly-plus signature) is
$$\mathcal L_\pi=f^2e^{2\pi}(\partial\pi)^2+\frac{f^3}{\Lambda^3}(\partial\pi)^2\Box\pi+\frac{f^3}{2\Lambda^3}(\partial\pi)^4 .$$
* On Minkowski space it has the solution $e^{\pi}=-1/H_0t$, with $H_0^2\propto\Lambda^3/f$.
* On that solution $\rho\simeq0$ and $p\propto-f^2/H_0^2t^4$.
* Coupled to gravity, $H=h/(-t)^3$ with $h\simeq f^2/(9M_P^2H_0^2)$ up to O(1) factors.

A laboratory route also needs three additions:
* (i) a second field, or explicit dilatation breaking, so that a *finite* seed interpolates to the Minkowski vacuum without strong coupling. Rubakov
  2013 shows that this is impossible in the simplest single-field model and gives a two-field scenario;
* (ii) a hand-over from the genesis phase to the daughter's vacuum energy (a false vacuum $U(\phi)$ as in Round 5);
* (iii) a weak portal to ordinary matter, so that the seed can be prepared.

### C3. Seed timeline (Proposition G6, Galilean-genesis scaling)
With $H=h/(-t)^3$ and $|p|=3hc^4/4\pi G(ct)^4$:
* anti-trapped spheres of radius $L$ appear when $H=c/L$, at $c|t_*|=(hL)^{1/3}$;
* the flat-space genesis background is still valid there if $(h/L^2)^{1/3}\ll1$;
* the budget ratio $|p_*|L^2/(c^4/8\pi G)=6(L^2/h)^{1/3}$ is then automatically larger than 1 (`genesis_timeline`).

Starting at stress $p_0=10^{11}$ Pa (the strength of the strongest materials, for scale only):

| $L$ | $h$ | $t_*$ | time from $p_0$ to daughter | amplification | validity $(h/L^2)^{1/3}$ | budget ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 m | $10^{-4}$ m² | 0.15 ns | 43 ms | $6\times10^{33}$ | 0.046 | 129 |
| 1 cm | $10^{-8}$ m² | 1.5 ps | 4.3 ms | $6\times10^{37}$ | 0.046 | 129 |
| 1 μm | $10^{-16}$ m² | 0.15 fs | 43 μs | $6\times10^{45}$ | 0.046 | 129 |

These numbers are scalings in a homogeneous patch. The boundary of a finite seed (C2(i)) and the hand-over (C2(ii)) are model-dependent and not
computed.

### C3b. Proposition G7 (the edge of a seed decides the outcome) — [Theorem in the thin-interface model + numerics]
Take an interior flat FRW genesis patch, $H=h/(-t)^3$ and $a=e^{h/2t^2}$, a comoving thin interface at $R=ar_c$, and a Schwarzschild exterior
of mass $M$.
* For a comoving wall in flat FRW, $A_{\rm in}^2=1-2m_{\rm in}/R+\dot R^2=1$ exactly, since $m_{\rm in}=R^3H^2/2G$ and $\dot R=HR$.
* The junction then gives two branches:
  $$\kappa R=1\mp\sqrt{1-2GM/R+H^2R^2}\qquad(\text{exterior orientation }\pm1).$$
* They meet only where the square root vanishes, which needs $R<2GM$ (G2).

Regular data start on the visible branch, and $\kappa$ turns negative at $HR=\sqrt{2GM/R}$. Numerically this happens at $HR=0.0448$, 0.249 and
0.450, against predicted values 0.0447, 0.258 and 0.447 (`genesis_interface`). In every case it happens before anti-trapping ($HR=1$), and the
interface ends with $\kappa R\to-HR$: a negative-energy wall driving a visible inflating region outward into the parent.

**Consequence.** A hidden, safe daughter needs the bridge to form in a region where the NEC is violated with the full budget of G1 along the
ingoing rays through the throat. The self-amplifying stress must therefore extend into an *inhomogeneous* boundary layer. This is Rubakov's
interpolation problem, and it now carries a second meaning: it decides between a daughter and a parent-consuming bubble. **It is the first
calculation Route G needs (P-R6b).**

### C4. What forms, and the safety floor
* The daughter forms behind a horizon of mass $M_{\rm ADM}$ (Birkhoff), the seed's total energy.
* The exterior black hole evaporates, releasing $M_{\rm ADM}c^2$ in $8.4\times10^{-17}\,{\rm s}\,(M/1\,{\rm kg})^3$.
* A semiclassical throat needs $M_{\rm ADM}\gg m_P$. So every event releases at least about 2 GJ (0.5 t TNT) at $M=m_P$, and 5 t at $10\,m_P$,
  in less than $10^{-35}$ s.
* Since Route G's ADM mass is set by the seed rather than by the daughter, the design target would be $M_{\rm ADM}$ as small as semiclassicality
  allows.
* These are characteristics of the theory, given as constraints for Round 9, not as a plan.

### C5. The gates: what must be discovered and how it could be tested
* (G-a) **Existence** of a sector with stable, self-amplifying NEC violation, with a portal to ordinary matter.
* (G-b) **The GSL.** A minimally coupled sector of this kind violates the classical area theorem. Its existence would be a laboratory or
  astrophysical violation of black-hole thermodynamics, testable in principle without making a universe. Dubovsky–Sibiryakov and
  Eling–Foster–Jacobson–Wall show the same tension for Lorentz-violating sectors with several propagation speeds.
* (G-c) **Positivity.** The sign structure Galileon-type genesis needs violates the forward-scattering positivity bounds (Adams et al. 2006). The
  sector's $2\to2$ amplitudes would show a negative $s^2$ coefficient.
* (G-d) **Causal structure.** Stable NEC violation requires superluminal modes around some backgrounds (Dubovsky–Grégoire–Nicolis–Rattazzi 2006).
  The subluminal genesis variants trade this for other restrictions.
* (G-e) **Preparability.** A finite seed with a weakly coupled boundary (Rubakov's two-field scenario), and a hand-over to inflation, are the
  first things to compute.

### C6. Event rate (task 6G)
* Inside the attractor basin of the genesis solution, formation is classical: **$P\approx1$ per seed**, and the event rate is the
  seed-preparation rate.
* The transition prediction is the formation time $\Delta t(L,h,p_0)$ and the saturation of G1 at $t_*$. These are quantitatively testable
  once $f,\Lambda,H_0$ are known.
* For comparison: Route 1 gives $\le10^{-23}$ per prepared Planck-density bubble, Route 2 gives $e^{-B}\omega$ for a stabilised Planck-density
  seed, and Route 6 gives 1 with an undetermined outcome.

### C7. Assessment
[Assessment] Theoretical priors are against Route G:
* positivity bounds and the GSL are among the best-supported structures in quantum field theory and black-hole physics;
* the 2016 no-go theorems (Libanov–Mironov–Rubakov; Kobayashi) already exclude complete non-singular histories in Horndeski theory, leaving
  beyond-Horndeski theories.

But the question is empirical, and Route G is the only route on which a discovery would change the requirements from Planckian to
laboratory scales. **It is the right target for Genesis-LAB.**

## Part D — The Round 6 tasks, answered
* **6A (does suppression persist in a full treatment?).** Within the reduced quantum theory, yes: Theorem A1 and Lemma A6 leave only an
  entropy weight, which cannot touch the double-flip class. Topology-changing (wormhole) channels are Planck-scale and uncomputed. No known
  full treatment lowers the frontier.
* **6B (other initial configurations).** Charged and rotating exteriors (classical beyond a Cauchy horizon), seed black holes (≤ 30%), closed
  or de Sitter parents (cosmological), stabilised seeds (Planck density).
* **6C (other matter or gravity).** NEC-violating walls are excluded (G2, G3). Bulk NEC violation with the GSL is excluded (Wall). Bulk NEC
  violation without the GSL is Route G. Low-scale gravity rescales energies but not suppression.
* **6D (does a method change the probability, or only relabel it?).** The criterion: a method changes the physical probability only if it
  changes the transition's parameters ($M,\rho,\sigma,Q$), the number of attempts (stabilisation), or the transition class (classical instead
  of quantum).
  * Clock choices (F17), post-selection and parallel attempts are relabelling.
  * Measurement-induced acceleration is energy injection, i.e. assistance (Prop. B1).
* **6E (regular initial conditions).** Routes 1–3, 6 and 12 start from regular data. Routes 6–8 do so only by passing a Cauchy horizon.
* **6F (singularity, energy and entropy constraints).** G1, G2, Wall's GSL theorem, strong cosmic censorship, vacuum stability (G3) and the
  safety floor (C4).
* **6G (event rate of the strongest candidate).** Route G: $P\approx1$ per seed, deterministic formation time (C3, C6). Strongest
  known-physics route: a stabilised seed, $\tau=e^B/\omega$ (A4).

## Part F — Failed approaches and open problems of this round
* **F19 (ghost pulse as a seed).** A massless ghost-sign scalar ($G\to-G$ in the matter terms of the Phase IV solver, zero potential) was imploded from
  flat data with amplitudes up to 2 (Planck units) and widths 0.15–0.3. No anti-trapped point appeared before the evolution ended near the
  centre, and $\max r_u<0$. G1 is necessary, not sufficient: NEC violation must be coherent and focused. The run was not pursued, since ghost
  matter destabilises the vacuum in any case.
* **F20 (breathing seeds in a coarse scan).** A scan over 96 wall-plus-dust and negative-tension laws found no stably oscillating orbit in our
  region compatible with a later bridge crossing. This is consistent with G2. It does not contradict Guendelman–Sakai, whose breathing orbits
  may lie outside the scanned range.
